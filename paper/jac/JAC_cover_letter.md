Dear Editor-in-Chief,

I submit **"Can current surveillance follow doxy-PEP-associated *Staphylococcus
aureus* resistance? A multi-stream evidence audit"** for consideration as an
Original Article.

**Why JAC.** Doxycycline post-exposure prophylaxis (doxy-PEP) is being deployed at
population scale to prevent bacterial STIs, applying sustained tetracycline
pressure to networks that historically amplified community *S. aureus*. The
pivotal trial's final randomised analysis has now reported a significant increase
in incident doxycycline-resistant *S. aureus* under doxy-PEP (hazard ratio 3.89,
95% CI 1.42–10.68), and its investigators called for public-health surveillance of
exactly this. This paper asks the operational question that call raises and that
sits squarely within JAC's scope of antimicrobial resistance, surveillance, and
stewardship: **can the guidance and surveillance now deployed around doxy-PEP
actually follow that signal?**

**What we find.** Auditing three evidence streams against one detectability
yardstick, we show that no governmental guideline requires *S. aureus* monitoring
(0 of 6 outbreak-matched jurisdictions; 0 of 10 coded), and that none of twelve
established US surveillance systems links doxy-PEP exposure to an *S. aureus*
tetracycline phenotype at a common population denominator (0 of 12) — largely
because staphylococcal surveillance is standardised around the methicillin axis
while the treatment-relevant unit here is tetracycline susceptibility. We further
show that an ordinary population system would need an implausible within-exposed
effect to detect a rate change, and we specify the sentinel architecture that
would close the gap. We make **no population-level causal claim**; the contribution
is an evidentiary-adequacy and surveillance-design result.

**Rigour and reproducibility.** The analysis uses only public data; every coded
source carries a locator and is SHA-256-pinned; analysis decisions were date-logged
before results were known; and every figure and table regenerates by a single
command from a public repository archived with a citable DOI. Supplementary data
(cited in the main article) hold the full coding protocols, system matrix, and
dilution derivation.
