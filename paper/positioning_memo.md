# Positioning memo — Gaspari (support vs occupy) and answering Mishriky & Chan

*2026-08-19. Feeds the Discussion and the exposure-intensity section.*

## 1. Gaspari 2026 — SUPPORTS the thesis; does NOT occupy it

Gaspari V, Foschi C, et al. *Sex Transm Infect* 2026 (`10.1136/sextrans-2026-056978`).
Bologna STI clinic; cross-sectional; **80 MSM on PrEP vs 73 HIV-negative MSM not on
PrEP**; nasal *S. aureus* culture + susceptibility.

Findings:
- Carriage did **not** differ (27%/… ; p=0.17) — consistent with Soge (carriage
  *lower* in exposed) and DoxyPEP (carriage fell).
- **Resistance in nasal *S. aureus* was higher among PrEP users**: erythromycin
  (p=0.0008), clindamycin (p=0.004), **tetracycline (p=0.03)**. Multidrug-resistant
  isolates **only** among PrEP users.
- **Tetracycline *exposure* — not PrEP status — was independently associated with
  tetracycline resistance** (p=0.03; "PrEP status per se was not").

**Why it supports and does not occupy:**
1. It is the **empirical bystander signal, detected** — nasal *S. aureus* tetR is
   elevated in the doxycycline-exposed, by **targeted clinic sampling** (n=153,
   exposure-stratified). That is exactly the design Stream C says works (high κ /
   sample the exposed directly) and population surveillance cannot. Gaspari
   *corroborates* Stream C rather than pre-empting it.
2. It does **not** make the paper's argument — that no population surveillance can
   detect this, that guidelines discuss-but-don't-measure it, or the three-stream
   evidentiary-adequacy framing. Gaspari is the biology arm (with Soge); the
   contribution here is the measurement/surveillance/institutional gap.
3. It **hands us the exposure-intensity argument** (see §3): the driver is the
   drug, dosed, not the label. Cite Gaspari + Soge as establishing the signal is
   real; position the novelty as *no one is instrumented to see it at scale*.

Risk to manage: Gaspari's abstract line "may be associated with antibiotic
exposure rather than PrEP use per se" could read as deflating doxy-PEP's role. It
does the opposite for us — doxy-PEP *is* population-scale tetracycline exposure, so
"the drug, not the label, drives it" is the exposure-intensity point, not an
exoneration of doxy-PEP.

## 2. Answering Mishriky & Chan 2026 (CID viewpoint)

Mishriky F, Chan PA. "Antimicrobial Resistance Concerns Should Not Limit Doxy-PEP
Implementation." *Clin Infect Dis* 2026 (`10.1093/cid/ciag075`). (Open Door Health /
Brown / RI PHI; note the adjacent ethics viewpoint is co-authored by the CDC
guideline lead, Bachmann.)

Their argument, in four moves, and the answer to each:

| Their move | Answer |
|---|---|
| **"Theoretical concerns of AMR should not limit implementation."** | The bystander *S. aureus* effect is **not theoretical, it is measured.** Soge: tetracycline-resistant *S. aureus* colonization **18% vs 8% (P<.0001)** in doxy-PEP users vs non-users, dose-dependent (>3 doses/month associated; any use not). Gaspari (2026, same journal ecosystem, three weeks before this memo): nasal *S. aureus* tetR higher in the exposed, exposure-driven (p=0.03). Calling it "theoretical" is empirically out of date. |
| **Animal-agriculture deflection** ("reduce tetracycline in animal feed instead"; 110,777 tons/yr). | Whataboutism. A larger tetracycline source elsewhere does not make the doxy-PEP bystander effect non-existent, and — crucially — it is not an argument against *measuring* it. The paper's claim is about measurability, not magnitude ranking. |
| **General-medicine overuse deflection.** | Same structure; orthogonal to whether the specific, dosed, in-population exposure is instrumented. |
| **"Monitoring should include local AMR *including N. gonorrhoeae*."** | This is the paper's finding stated by the opposition. Their monitoring proposal is **in-category only** — the exact asymmetry the corpus documents. A CDC-guideline-adjacent viewpoint arguing *for* implementation still proposes measuring only the in-category organism. The counter-camp is itself Stream B data. And "monitoring will catch it" is unfounded for the bystander: Stream C shows population surveillance cannot detect it, and no stream samples *S. aureus* at all. |

**Discussion stance:** we do not dispute efficacy (chlamydia/syphilis; CLAUDE
constraint — not an anti-doxy-PEP paper). We dispute the "theoretical" and
"monitoring will catch it" claims. The strongest single sentence: *the risk its
own proponents call theoretical was measured at 18% vs 8% by the study they cite
for gonococcal resistance, and their remedy — monitor the in-category organism —
is the very omission this paper quantifies.*

## 3. Exposure-intensity thread (feeds that writing section)

Three independent sources say the exposure that matters is **cumulative
drug-intensity, not binary status/prescription** — the unit population
surveillance structurally cannot record:
- **Soge**: *any* use not associated (RR 1.14); **>3 doses/month** associated
  (RR 1.42). Dose threshold, not status.
- **Gaspari**: tetracycline *exposure* associated; **PrEP status was not**.
- **WHO** guideline concedes doxy-PEP's benefit turns on a "limited range of
  pathogens" / context-dependent resistance — the effect is graded, not binary.
- **Kenyon change-point / consumption correspondence** quantify population
  antibiotic-*consumption* (drug-days), the right exposure metric.

The measurement-inheritance point lands here: guidelines and surveillance record a
**binary prescription**, but the biology responds to **cumulative drug-days**, and
the bystander effect is dose-dependent — so the instrument is mis-specified at the
exposure end as well as the outcome end. Stream C's own model already uses a
dose-above-threshold adjustment; Kenyon's change-point analysis is the template for
upgrading that from a point estimate to a distribution.
