## Abstract

**Background.** Doxycycline post-exposure prophylaxis (doxy-PEP) has been scaled
across sexual-health systems since citywide (San Francisco, October 2022) and
national (CDC, 2024) guidance. Doxycycline selects for tetracycline resistance
beyond its target organism, *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*; *Staphylococcus aureus* is a
plausible **bystander** — a commensal and pathogen that carries tetracycline
resistance determinants yet sits outside the sexually-transmitted-infection
surveillance frame. We asked whether the association between population doxy-PEP
uptake and tetracycline-resistant *S. aureus* is detectable with existing public
surveillance, before acquiring any outcome data.

**Methods.** We specify the intended design — a controlled interrupted time
series on calendar time, guideline release as the exogenous interruption, high-
versus low-PrEP-density geographies as the contrast — and then subject it to a
feasibility gate. Using AIDSVu state PrEP data (2012–2025, public) as a proxy for
doxy-PEP exposure density, we model the state- and metro-level tetracycline
non-susceptibility rate as a two-component mixture of exposed and unexposed
*S. aureus* isolates and compute the within-exposed effect that would be required
to move the observed population rate detectably. All code and data are public and
every figure regenerates from a single command.

**Results.** The exposed subgroup is too dilute to detect at any geographic grain
that has a population denominator. At the state level, the within-exposed
relative risk required for a detectable shift is a median of ~14 across a
generous sensitivity grid (best case 1.9), versus the optimistic RR of 1.42
observed by Soge et al.; 0 of 81 scenarios are detectable. At the metro level —
where uptake is densest — detection would require male-PrEP density from 2.1× to
nearly 500× that of the densest geography that exists in the United States
(Washington, D.C.), and
in proxy-free units would require an implausible-to-impossible share of *all*
adult males on PrEP (325% under realistic isolate volumes; 6.5% even in a
fantastical best case, versus D.C.'s observed 2.7%). The verdict is robust to the
modeling assumptions.

**Conclusions.** The association, if real, is undetectable by population-scale
ecological surveillance because no such system samples the concentrated exposed
population; only targeted clinic sampling — a cohort design, not surveillance —
could recover it. This is a quantitative demonstration of *measurement
inheritance*: surveillance instrumented for an in-category organism cannot detect
a bystander signal it was never built to see. **This link is ecological and rests
on two proxy layers — geographic PrEP density proxies doxy-PEP uptake, which
proxies cumulative population doxycycline-days — and no claim here is causal.**

## 1. Introduction

Doxy-PEP — a single 200 mg dose of doxycycline taken after condomless sex —
reduces bacterial STIs in men who have sex with men (MSM) and transgender women
and has been adopted rapidly since the DoxyPEP trial [@luetkemeyer2023] and
subsequent guidance. Because doxycycline is a tetracycline, its population-scale
use exerts selection pressure that is not confined to *N. gonorrhoeae*.
Tetracycline resistance in gonococci is already near-saturating in exposed
populations [@schroder2025], which bounds the drug's antigonococcal benefit and
sharpens attention on what else the exposure selects.

*S. aureus* is the natural bystander. It colonizes the same individuals, carries
mobile tetracycline-resistance determinants, and — unlike *N. gonorrhoeae* — is
not tracked by any STI-oriented surveillance apparatus. Soge et al. measured it
directly, finding tetracycline-resistant *S. aureus* colonization associated with
higher-intensity doxy-PEP use [@soge2025]. The concern here is therefore **not**
that the field ignored *S. aureus* (it did not: DuDHS, DoxyPEP, and Soge all
assayed it), and **not** that doxy-PEP "causes MRSA" (methicillin resistance in
this population is too rare to power anything — Soge found 12/838 isolates, 1%).

The claim we examine is narrower and structural: **cumulative doxycycline
exposure at population scale is associated with tetracycline-resistant
*S. aureus*, and no existing surveillance system is instrumented to detect that
association.** Two features of the measurement apparatus produce the blind spot.
First, exposure is recorded as a binary prescription rather than as cumulative
drug-days, so the dose–response that Soge observed — *any* use was not associated
with resistance (RR 1.14), but >3 doses/month was (RR 1.42) — is invisible to
prescription-anchored designs [@soge2025]. This is the same misspecification our
companion correspondence identifies in a published doxy-PEP interrupted time
series, where the interruption is a prescription date rather than an exogenous
event [@demidont2026cid]. Second, the bystander organism is monitored with far
less apparatus than the in-category organism: gonococcal resistance has dedicated
sentinel programs; tetracycline non-susceptibility in *S. aureus* from this
population has none.

We call this *measurement inheritance*: an analysis inherits the sampling frame,
cadence, and coding of the surveillance it is built on, and those were chosen for
a different organism and a different question. Rather than assert the blind spot,
we quantify it. Before acquiring a single outcome datum, we ask whether the
signal *could* be detected with the surveillance that exists — and show it cannot.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Intended design

The design we set out to run is a controlled interrupted time series (ITS) on
calendar time, with an **exogenous** interruption — the citywide (October 2022)
or national (2024) guideline release, which applies to everyone in a geography at
once — never individual initiation, which conflates a prescription with an event
[@demidont2026cid; @lopezbernal2018]. The contrast is high- versus
low-PrEP-density geographies, both arms on one clock, with a continuous exposure
intensity proxy rather than a binary contrast (a binary contrast is expected to
be null, per Soge). Pre-period slopes would be tested for parallelism, and three
negative controls (methicillin resistance; cisgender women, for whom doxy-PEP is
not recommended; a non-tetracycline phenotype) would be required.

The **outcome** is tetracycline non-susceptibility among *S. aureus* isolates —
not MRSA. A caveat travels with it: tetK (efflux) confers tetracycline resistance
while leaving doxycycline active, whereas tetM (ribosomal protection) does not. In
a doxy-PEP-eligible population, 13.2% of isolates were tetracycline-resistant but
only 2.0% doxycycline-resistant. Surveillance that reports "tetracycline"
susceptibility is therefore loosely coupled to the drug actually taken, and any
outcome variable must carry that caveat.

### 2.2 Exposure data

We use AIDSVu state-level PrEP data, annual 2012–2025 (public) [@aidsvu]: PrEP
users and rate, male PrEP users and rate, and the PrEP-to-Need ratio, for 52
geographies (50 states, DC, Puerto Rico). Male PrEP density is the better proxy,
as doxy-PEP is recommended for MSM and transgender women and explicitly not for
cisgender women. Population denominators are recovered internally from AIDSVu
(users ÷ rate × 10⁵) rather than imported, keeping numerator and denominator
consistent. Metro-level PrEP exposure is not available as a public isolate-linked
stream; we address the metro case analytically (§2.4).

### 2.3 The feasibility gate

The observed population-level tetracycline non-susceptibility rate is a mixture
of exposed and unexposed isolates. If doxy-PEP multiplies the exposed subgroup's
resistance rate by RR, the induced shift in the observed rate is

$$\Delta R = f \cdot R_0 \cdot (RR - 1) \qquad (1)$$

where $R_0$ is baseline tetracycline non-susceptibility, RR is the within-exposed
relative risk, and $f$ is the fraction of the geography's *S. aureus* isolates
originating from the exposed. The dilution fraction is

$$f = \frac{\text{male PrEP users} \times \text{uptake} \times \kappa}{\text{population}} \qquad (2)$$

with uptake the doxy-PEP uptake among PrEP users (bounded 20–55%; 55% is a San
Francisco sexual-health-clinic high-water mark, not a national value) and $\kappa$
an isolate-enrichment factor (κ = 1 is proportional sampling). With $N$ isolates
per geography-year, the minimum detectable absolute change in a proportion near
$R_0$ (two-sided α = 0.05, 80% power) is

$$\text{MDE} = (z_{\alpha/2} + z_{\text{power}})\sqrt{2 R_0 (1-R_0)/N} \qquad (3)$$

and the within-exposed RR that would be *needed* for detection is obtained by
inverting (1):

$$RR_\text{needed} = 1 + \frac{\text{MDE}}{f \cdot R_0}. \qquad (4)$$

The gate fails if $RR_\text{needed}$ exceeds the optimistic observed effect
(RR = 1.42) across a sensitivity grid. Parameters are chosen to *favour*
detection so that a failed gate is robust: uptake ∈ {20, 35, 55}%, κ ∈ {1, 3, 5},
$R_0$ ∈ {5, 10, 13}%, and — deliberately — isolate volumes $N$ ∈ {1 000, 10 000,
100 000} per geography-year, where 100 000 exceeds any real US surveillance
stream. Soge's RR 1.42 (>3 doses/month) is used as the optimistic ceiling
[@soge2025].

### 2.4 The metro pivot, analytically

The dilution fraction is population-*independent* — it depends on exposure
*density*, not headcount:

$$f = \phi \cdot \frac{\text{male PrEP rate}}{10^5} \cdot \text{uptake} \cdot \kappa \qquad (5)$$

with $\phi$ the adult-male share of the isolate-generating population (≈0.5). The
metro question is therefore whether any real metro's male-PrEP density is high
enough. We invert (4) to the required density and compare it against the densest
geography that exists in AIDSVu — Washington, D.C. (2 694 per 100 000 adult males,
2022) — a city-state and a generous empirical ceiling for any US metro (San
Francisco and King County do not exceed it). No metro-specific PrEP file exists
as a public isolate-linked resource; D.C. therefore serves as the observed
high-water mark, and requirements are expressed as multiples of it and, more
transparently, as a share of *all* adult males.

### 2.5 Reproducibility

All analysis is Python 3.11 (pandas, statsmodels, matplotlib); there are no
proprietary dependencies. Raw data are immutable; all cleaning is in code; every
figure and table regenerates from `make all` with no manual steps. Analysis
decisions that could go either way are logged with dates in the repository before
results are known. The repository is public with code and data — a standard the
companion 150-journal audit found essentially no journal requires
[@demidont2026metaarxiv].

## 3. Results

### 3.1 State level

Across the full sensitivity grid, **0 of 81 scenarios are detectable**. The
within-exposed RR required to move the state-level rate is a median of ~14 —
roughly ten times Soge's optimistic 1.42. Even the single most detection-
favourable cell (largest dilution, smallest requirement) needs RR = 1.9, above
the ceiling; under a realistic cell (proportional sampling, 1 000 isolates/year,
uptake 35%) it needs RR ≈ 86, ~60× the observed effect. Notably, the most
favourable "state" is the city-state D.C., which itself points toward the metro
pivot. (Figure 1; `outputs/feasibility_result.md`.)

### 3.2 Metro level

The pivot does not rescue the design. Detection would require male-PrEP density
from 2.1× (most favourable cell) to ~480× (realistic cells) that of D.C. — the
densest US geography — and 0 of 81 scenarios are achievable within a generous 2×
ceiling. In proxy-free units, the male-PrEP
coverage required to make the signal detectable is **325% of all adult males**
under realistic isolate volumes (impossible) and **6.5% even in the fantastical
best case** (100 000 isolates/year, 5× enrichment, 55% uptake), versus D.C.'s
observed 2.7% and the single-digit-percent MSM share of any real male population.
(Figures 2–3; `outputs/feasibility_metro_result.md`.)

### 3.3 Robustness

The verdict is not an artifact of the modeling knobs. Sweeping the adult-male
share (0.4–0.6) and the "achievable" density ceiling (1–5× D.C.) across the full
grid, the achievable-cell count stays 0 at any realistic ceiling (≤2× D.C.).
Cells begin to open only under a *compound* of implausibilities — a metro 3–5×
denser than any US geography that exists, **and** the fantastical isolate volume
of 100 000/year, **and** κ ≥ 3 — and even then at most 11 of 81. Every such cell's
high κ is targeted clinic sampling, i.e. a cohort design, not the ecological one
under test. (`outputs/tables/feasibility_metro_robustness.csv`.)

## 4. Discussion

The association between doxy-PEP and tetracycline-resistant *S. aureus*, if it
exists, cannot be detected by population-scale ecological surveillance at any
geographic grain that carries a population denominator. The reason is not a weak
effect but a sampling mismatch: the exposed are a small, geographically
concentrated subgroup, and surveillance samples the whole population. The only
lever in equation (2) that recovers detectability is enrichment, κ — and high κ
is exactly targeted sampling of *S. aureus* from the exposed population, which is
a cohort or sentinel-clinic design, not surveillance. King County's doxy-PEP-era
sexual-health-clinic isolate collection is that design; it is where the signal
lives, and it is not what population surveillance provides.

This is measurement inheritance made quantitative. A surveillance system encodes,
in its sampling frame and its variables, the organism and question it was built
for. Gonococcal resistance is tracked intensively because it is *in category*;
the bystander *S. aureus* signal is diluted below detectability precisely because
it is not. The exposure, too, is inherited in the wrong units — a binary
prescription where the biology responds to cumulative drug-days [@soge2025] — the
same misspecification that undermines prescription-anchored ITS designs in this
literature [@demidont2026cid]. The blind spot is not an oversight to be corrected
by better analysis of existing data; it is a property of the instrument.

What would it take to see the signal? The calculation names the instrumentation:
sentinel sampling of *S. aureus* from doxy-PEP-exposed individuals (raising κ by
construction), capture of dosing frequency rather than prescription status, and a
bystander-organism panel run on the same specimens as gonococcal surveillance.
None of these is exotic; none exists at scale. That absence — not any single
estimate — is the finding.

## 5. Limitations

The link is ecological and rests on two proxy layers: geographic PrEP density
proxies doxy-PEP uptake, which proxies cumulative population doxycycline-days.
*S. aureus* isolates carry no sexual-behaviour tag. No outcome data were acquired
— by design: the feasibility gate failed first, and acquiring surveillance data to
estimate an undetectable signal would be effort spent to confirm a foregone null.
Metro exposure uses D.C. as an empirical ceiling because no metro/county PrEP
density is available as a public isolate-linked resource; obtaining real
San Francisco / King County / Los Angeles / New York county densities would
sharpen, but cannot overturn, a conclusion that already holds by orders of
magnitude. The "tetracycline" outcome is loosely coupled to doxycycline via the
tetK/tetM distinction, which would attenuate any real effect further. Modeling
assumptions were bounded generously toward detection; the negative verdict is
robust to them (§3.3).

## 6. What would change this conclusion

Consistent with a falsification standard: the conclusion would change if a
surveillance stream emerged that samples the exposed population directly (high
κ), or if isolate volumes with exposure linkage rose by orders of magnitude, or
if the true within-exposed RR were far above the observed 1.42. And should such
data ever permit the intended ITS, the hypothesis itself is falsifiable: if
tetracycline resistance in *S. aureus* is flat or declining in high-PrEP-density
geographies after the guideline interruption while the negative controls behave,
the hypothesis is wrong, and that is a publishable result. The present paper's
claim is prior to and independent of that test: with the surveillance that
exists, the test cannot be run.
