\begin{center}\textbf{Abstract}\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{Background.} Doxycycline post-exposure prophylaxis (doxy-PEP)
has been scaled across sexual-health systems since citywide (San Francisco,
October 2022) and national (CDC, 2024) guidance~\cite{cdc2024,sfdph2022}.
Doxycycline selects for tetracycline resistance beyond its target organism,
\textit{Neisseria gonorrhoeae}; \textit{Staphylococcus aureus} is a plausible
\textbf{bystander}---a commensal and pathogen that carries tetracycline-resistance
determinants yet sits outside the sexually-transmitted-infection surveillance
frame. We asked whether the association between population doxy-PEP uptake and
tetracycline-resistant \textit{S.\ aureus} is detectable with existing public
surveillance, before acquiring any outcome data.

\noindent\textbf{Methods.} We specify the intended design---a controlled
interrupted time series (ITS) on calendar time, guideline release as the
exogenous interruption, high- versus low-PrEP-density geographies as the
contrast---and subject it to a feasibility gate. Using AIDSVu state PrEP data
(2012--2025, public)~\cite{aidsvu} as a proxy for doxy-PEP exposure density, we
model the state- and metro-level tetracycline non-susceptibility rate as a
two-component mixture of exposed and unexposed \textit{S.\ aureus} isolates and
compute the within-exposed effect required to move the observed population rate
detectably. All code and data are public and every figure regenerates from a
single command.

\noindent\textbf{Results.} The exposed subgroup is too dilute to detect at any
geographic grain that has a population denominator. At the state level, the
within-exposed relative risk (RR) required for a detectable shift is a median of
${\sim}14$ across a generous sensitivity grid (best case 1.9), versus the
optimistic RR of 1.42 observed by Soge et al.~\cite{soge2025}; 0 of 81 scenarios
are detectable. At the metro level---where uptake is densest---detection would
require male-PrEP density from 2.1$\times$ (single most favourable cell) to nearly
500$\times$ that of the densest geography that exists in the United States
(Washington, D.C.), and in proxy-free units would require an implausible-to-
impossible share of \emph{all} adult males on PrEP (325\% under realistic isolate
volumes; 6.5\% even in a fantastical best case, versus D.C.'s observed 2.7\%). The
verdict is robust to the modeling assumptions.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusions.} The association, if real, is undetectable by
population-scale ecological surveillance because no such system samples the
concentrated exposed population; only targeted clinic sampling---a cohort design,
not surveillance---could recover it. This is a quantitative demonstration of
\emph{measurement inheritance}: surveillance instrumented for an in-category
organism cannot detect a bystander signal it was never built to see.
\textbf{This link is ecological and rests on two proxy layers---geographic PrEP
density proxies doxy-PEP uptake, which proxies cumulative population
doxycycline-days---and no claim here is causal.}

%------------------------------------------------------------------ INTRODUCTION
\section{Introduction}

Doxy-PEP---a single 200~mg dose of doxycycline taken after condomless sex---reduces
bacterial STIs in men who have sex with men (MSM) and transgender women and has
been adopted rapidly since the DoxyPEP trial~\cite{luetkemeyer2023} and subsequent
guidance. Because doxycycline is a tetracycline, its population-scale use exerts
selection pressure that is not confined to \textit{N.\ gonorrhoeae}. Tetracycline
resistance in gonococci is already near-saturating in exposed
populations~\cite{schroder2025}, which bounds the drug's antigonococcal benefit
and sharpens attention on what else the exposure selects.

\textit{S.\ aureus} is the natural bystander. It colonizes the same individuals,
carries mobile tetracycline-resistance determinants, and---unlike
\textit{N.\ gonorrhoeae}---is not tracked by any STI-oriented surveillance
apparatus. Soge et al.\ measured it directly, finding tetracycline-resistant
\textit{S.\ aureus} colonization associated with higher-intensity doxy-PEP
use~\cite{soge2025}. The concern here is therefore \textbf{not} that the field
ignored \textit{S.\ aureus} (it did not: DuDHS, DoxyPEP, and Soge all assayed it),
and \textbf{not} that doxy-PEP ``causes MRSA'' (methicillin resistance in this
population is too rare to power anything---Soge found 12/838 isolates, 1\%).

The claim we examine is narrower and structural: \textbf{cumulative doxycycline
exposure at population scale is associated with tetracycline-resistant
\textit{S.\ aureus}, and no existing surveillance system is instrumented to detect
that association.} Two features of the measurement apparatus produce the blind
spot. First, exposure is recorded as a binary prescription rather than as
cumulative drug-days, so the dose--response Soge observed---\emph{any} use was not
associated with resistance (RR 1.14), but $>$3 doses/month was (RR
1.42)---is invisible to prescription-anchored designs~\cite{soge2025}. This is the
same misspecification our companion correspondence identifies in a published
doxy-PEP interrupted time series, where the interruption is a prescription date
rather than an exogenous event~\cite{demidontcid,spinelli2026}. Second, the
bystander organism is monitored with far less apparatus than the in-category
organism: gonococcal resistance has dedicated sentinel programs; tetracycline
non-susceptibility in \textit{S.\ aureus} from this population has none.

We call this \emph{measurement inheritance}: an analysis inherits the sampling
frame, cadence, and coding of the surveillance it is built on, and those were
chosen for a different organism and a different question. Rather than assert the
blind spot, we quantify it. Before acquiring a single outcome datum, we ask
whether the signal \emph{could} be detected with the surveillance that exists---and
show it cannot.

%----------------------------------------------------------------------- METHODS
\section{Methods}

\subsection{Intended design}

The design we set out to run is a controlled ITS on calendar time, with an
\textbf{exogenous} interruption---the citywide (October 2022) or national (2024)
guideline release, which applies to everyone in a geography at once---never
individual initiation, which conflates a prescription with an
event~\cite{demidontcid,lopezbernal2018}. The contrast is high- versus
low-PrEP-density geographies, both arms on one clock, with a continuous exposure
intensity proxy rather than a binary contrast (a binary contrast is expected to be
null, per Soge). Pre-period slopes would be tested for parallelism, and three
negative controls (methicillin resistance; cisgender women, for whom doxy-PEP is
not recommended; a non-tetracycline phenotype) would be required.

The \textbf{outcome} is tetracycline non-susceptibility among \textit{S.\ aureus}
isolates---not MRSA. A caveat travels with it: \textit{tetK} (efflux) confers
tetracycline resistance while leaving doxycycline active, whereas \textit{tetM}
(ribosomal protection) does not. In a doxy-PEP-eligible population, 13.2\% of
isolates were tetracycline-resistant but only 2.0\% doxycycline-resistant.
Surveillance that reports ``tetracycline'' susceptibility is therefore loosely
coupled to the drug actually taken, and any outcome variable must carry that
caveat.

\subsection{Exposure data}

We use AIDSVu state-level PrEP data, annual 2012--2025
(public)~\cite{aidsvu}: PrEP users and rate, male PrEP users and rate, and the
PrEP-to-Need ratio, for 52 geographies (50 states, DC, Puerto Rico). Male PrEP
density is the better proxy, as doxy-PEP is recommended for MSM and transgender
women and explicitly not for cisgender women. Population denominators are
recovered internally from AIDSVu (users $\div$ rate $\times 10^5$) rather than
imported, keeping numerator and denominator consistent. Metro-level PrEP exposure
is not available as a public isolate-linked stream; we address the metro case
analytically (Section~\ref{sec:metro}).

\subsection{The feasibility gate}

The observed population-level tetracycline non-susceptibility rate is a mixture of
exposed and unexposed isolates. If doxy-PEP multiplies the exposed subgroup's
resistance rate by RR, the induced shift in the observed rate is
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:induced}
  \Delta R = f \cdot R_0 \cdot (RR - 1)
\end{equation}
%
where $R_0$ is baseline tetracycline non-susceptibility, RR is the within-exposed
relative risk, and $f$ is the fraction of the geography's \textit{S.\ aureus}
isolates originating from the exposed. The dilution fraction is
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dilution}
  f = \frac{(\text{male PrEP users}) \times \text{uptake} \times \kappa}{\text{population}}
\end{equation}
%
with uptake the doxy-PEP uptake among PrEP users (bounded 20--55\%; 55\% is a San
Francisco sexual-health-clinic high-water mark, not a national value) and $\kappa$
an isolate-enrichment factor ($\kappa = 1$ is proportional sampling). With $N$
isolates per geography-year, the minimum detectable absolute change in a
proportion near $R_0$ (two-sided $\alpha = 0.05$, 80\% power) is
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mde}
  \mathrm{MDE} = (z_{\alpha/2} + z_{\text{power}})\sqrt{2 R_0 (1-R_0)/N}
\end{equation}
%
and the within-exposed RR that would be \emph{needed} for detection follows by
inverting \eqref{eq:induced}:
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rrneeded}
  RR_{\text{needed}} = 1 + \frac{\mathrm{MDE}}{f \cdot R_0}.
\end{equation}
%
The gate fails if $RR_{\text{needed}}$ exceeds the optimistic observed effect
(RR $= 1.42$) across a sensitivity grid. Parameters are chosen to \emph{favour}
detection so that a failed gate is robust: uptake $\in \{20, 35, 55\}\%$,
$\kappa \in \{1, 3, 5\}$, $R_0 \in \{5, 10, 13\}\%$, and---deliberately---isolate
volumes $N \in \{1\,000, 10\,000, 100\,000\}$ per geography-year, where
$100\,000$ exceeds any real US surveillance stream. Soge's RR 1.42
($>$3 doses/month) is the optimistic ceiling~\cite{soge2025}.

\subsection{The metro pivot, analytically}\label{sec:metro}

The dilution fraction is population-\emph{independent}---it depends on exposure
\emph{density}, not headcount:
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density}
  f = \phi \cdot \frac{\text{male PrEP rate}}{10^5} \cdot \text{uptake} \cdot \kappa
\end{equation}
%
with $\phi$ the adult-male share of the isolate-generating population
($\approx 0.5$). The metro question is therefore whether any real metro's
male-PrEP density is high enough. We invert \eqref{eq:rrneeded} to the required
density and compare it against the densest geography that exists in
AIDSVu---Washington, D.C.\ (2\,694 per 100\,000 adult males, 2022)---a city-state
and a generous empirical ceiling for any US metro (San Francisco and King County
do not exceed it). No metro-specific PrEP file exists as a public isolate-linked
resource; D.C.\ therefore serves as the observed high-water mark, and requirements
are expressed as multiples of it and, more transparently, as a share of \emph{all}
adult males.

\subsection{Reproducibility}

All analysis is Python 3.11 (pandas, statsmodels, matplotlib); there are no
proprietary dependencies. Raw data are immutable; all cleaning is in code; every
figure and table regenerates from \texttt{make all} with no manual steps. Analysis
decisions that could go either way are logged with dates in the repository before
results are known. The repository is public with code and data---a standard the
companion 150-journal audit found essentially no journal
requires~\cite{demidontmetaarxiv}.

%----------------------------------------------------------------------- RESULTS
\section{Results}

\subsection{State level}

Across the full sensitivity grid, \textbf{0 of 81 scenarios are detectable}. The
within-exposed RR required to move the state-level rate is a median of ${\sim}14$
---roughly ten times Soge's optimistic 1.42. Even the single most detection-
favourable cell needs RR $= 1.9$, above the ceiling; under a realistic cell
(proportional sampling, 1\,000 isolates/year, uptake 35\%) it needs
RR $\approx 86$, ${\sim}60\times$ the observed effect. Notably, the most
favourable ``state'' is the city-state D.C., which itself points toward the metro
pivot (Figure~\ref{fig:state}).

\subsection{Metro level}

The pivot does not rescue the design. Detection would require male-PrEP density
from 2.1$\times$ (most favourable cell) to ${\sim}480\times$ (realistic cells)
that of D.C.---the densest US geography---and 0 of 81 scenarios are achievable
within a generous 2$\times$ ceiling. In proxy-free units, the male-PrEP coverage
required is \textbf{325\% of all adult males} under realistic isolate volumes
(impossible) and \textbf{6.5\% even in the fantastical best case} (100\,000
isolates/year, 5$\times$ enrichment, 55\% uptake), versus D.C.'s observed 2.7\%
and the single-digit-percent MSM share of any real male population
(Figures~\ref{fig:metro}--\ref{fig:breakeven}).

\subsection{Robustness}

The verdict is not an artifact of the modeling knobs. Sweeping the adult-male
share (0.4--0.6) and the ``achievable'' density ceiling (1--5$\times$ D.C.) across
the full grid, the achievable-cell count stays 0 at any realistic ceiling
($\le 2\times$ D.C.). Cells begin to open only under a \emph{compound} of
implausibilities---a metro 3--5$\times$ denser than any US geography that exists,
\textbf{and} the fantastical isolate volume of 100\,000/year, \textbf{and}
$\kappa \ge 3$---and even then at most 11 of 81. Every such cell's high $\kappa$ is
targeted clinic sampling, i.e.\ a cohort design, not the ecological one under test.

%-------------------------------------------------------------------- DISCUSSION
\section{Discussion}

The association between doxy-PEP and tetracycline-resistant \textit{S.\ aureus}, if
it exists, cannot be detected by population-scale ecological surveillance at any
geographic grain that carries a population denominator. The reason is not a weak
effect but a sampling mismatch: the exposed are a small, geographically
concentrated subgroup, and surveillance samples the whole population. The only
lever in \eqref{eq:dilution} that recovers detectability is enrichment,
$\kappa$---and high $\kappa$ is exactly targeted sampling of \textit{S.\ aureus}
from the exposed population, which is a cohort or sentinel-clinic design, not
surveillance. King County's doxy-PEP-era sexual-health-clinic isolate collection
is that design; it is where the signal lives, and it is not what population
surveillance provides.

This is measurement inheritance made quantitative. A surveillance system encodes,
in its sampling frame and its variables, the organism and question it was built
for. Gonococcal resistance is tracked intensively because it is \emph{in
category}; the bystander \textit{S.\ aureus} signal is diluted below detectability
precisely because it is not. The exposure, too, is inherited in the wrong
units---a binary prescription where the biology responds to cumulative
drug-days~\cite{soge2025}---the same misspecification that undermines
prescription-anchored ITS designs in this literature~\cite{demidontcid}. The blind
spot is not an oversight to be corrected by better analysis of existing data; it is
a property of the instrument.

What would it take to see the signal? The calculation names the instrumentation:
sentinel sampling of \textit{S.\ aureus} from doxy-PEP-exposed individuals
(raising $\kappa$ by construction), capture of dosing frequency rather than
prescription status, and a bystander-organism panel run on the same specimens as
gonococcal surveillance. None of these is exotic; none exists at scale. That
absence---not any single estimate---is the finding.

%------------------------------------------------------------------- LIMITATIONS
\section{Limitations}

The link is ecological and rests on two proxy layers: geographic PrEP density
proxies doxy-PEP uptake, which proxies cumulative population doxycycline-days.
\textit{S.\ aureus} isolates carry no sexual-behaviour tag. No outcome data were
acquired---by design: the feasibility gate failed first, and acquiring
surveillance data to estimate an undetectable signal would be effort spent to
confirm a foregone null. Metro exposure uses D.C.\ as an empirical ceiling because
no metro/county PrEP density is available as a public isolate-linked resource;
obtaining real San Francisco / King County / Los Angeles / New York county
densities would sharpen, but cannot overturn, a conclusion that already holds by
orders of magnitude. The ``tetracycline'' outcome is loosely coupled to
doxycycline via the \textit{tetK}/\textit{tetM} distinction, which would attenuate
any real effect further. Modeling assumptions were bounded generously toward
detection; the negative verdict is robust to them (Section~3.3).

%------------------------------------------------------- FALSIFICATION
\section{What would change this conclusion}

Consistent with a falsification standard: the conclusion would change if a
surveillance stream emerged that samples the exposed population directly (high
$\kappa$), or if isolate volumes with exposure linkage rose by orders of
magnitude, or if the true within-exposed RR were far above the observed 1.42. And
should such data ever permit the intended ITS, the hypothesis itself is
falsifiable: if tetracycline resistance in \textit{S.\ aureus} is flat or
declining in high-PrEP-density geographies after the guideline interruption while
the negative controls behave, the hypothesis is wrong, and that is a publishable
result. The present paper's claim is prior to and independent of that test: with
the surveillance that exists, the test cannot be run.
