\section*{Abstract}
Downstream evidence systems --- guidelines, then surveillance --- can follow an upstream trial
signal only if they inherit the dimensions on which it was defined: the estimand, denominator,
unit, cadence, and linkage. We call the failure of that transmission measurement
inheritance and study it here. Doxycycline post-exposure prophylaxis (doxy-PEP) reduces bacterial sexually transmitted infections (STIs) and has scaled into guidance at every level; doxycycline also selects for tetracycline
resistance beyond its target, and \emph{Staphylococcus aureus} --- a commensal and pathogen for
which doxycycline is one of few oral treatments --- is the natural bystander. The pivotal
trial's final analysis now demonstrates a signal: among participants free of it at baseline,
doxy-PEP raised the hazard of incident doxycycline-resistant \emph{S. aureus} 3.89-fold
(95\% confidence interval [CI] 1.42--10.68; 68/393 vs 5/163), while colonization clearance did not differ (HR 1.01), and
its authors call for surveillance of it. We ask not whether doxy-PEP drives population-level
resistance, but whether the guidance and surveillance in place could follow the signal the
trial resolved. Auditing the evidence base in three streams against one yardstick --- the
within-exposed effect each could detect --- we find that no governmental guideline required \emph{S. aureus} measurement (0 of 6 outbreak-matched jurisdictions), and that of twelve established surveillance systems
none links doxy-PEP exposure to an \emph{S. aureus} tetracycline phenotype at a common population
denominator. The upstream instrument generated information the downstream systems are not built to
receive. The failure is also distributive: the benefit is estimable in recipients, while any
externality would fall on people outside the trial's frame and is joined to no denominator --- so one
side of the policy ledger is far more measurable than the other, an asymmetry we call distributive
observability. We make no population-level causal claim.

\section*{Introduction}

Doxycycline post-exposure prophylaxis reduces bacterial sexually
transmitted infections substantially \cite{luetkemeyer2023}, and has
moved quickly from trial result into guidance at every level, from the
World Health Organization through national and municipal health
departments \cite{cdc2024doxypep, sfdph2022}.

Every dose is selective pressure, and not only on the organisms it
targets. \emph{Staphylococcus aureus} is the consequential bystander: it
colonizes the anatomical sites these trials swabbed, and it carries
mobile tetracycline determinants \cite{grossman2016}.

Clinicians who prescribe doxy-PEP have registered the concern directly:
in a survey of thirty-six Italian STI-clinic dermatologists, 91.7\%
named MRSA selection as a worry, tied with gonococcal resistance as the
highest-rated in the study \cite{dona2026}.

The trial literature has not so much echoed the dermatologists' concern
as answered a narrower version of it. The US DoxyPEP trial cultured
\emph{S. aureus} and reported three distinct quantities: colonization
fell in the doxy-PEP arm (unsurprising for an active anti-staphylococcal
drug); doxycycline resistance among cultured isolates rose; and
resistance within the small methicillin-resistant subset stayed low
\cite{luetkemeyer2023croi}. The interim summary emphasized the last of
these --- MRSA resistance low and unchanged --- over the broader
\emph{S. aureus} measure, in which it rose. Carriage and resistance are
different quantities with different behavior, and the trial's own final
analysis has since drawn the line empirically: no effect on clearing
carriage (HR 1.01) but a significant increase in incident resistance (HR
3.89).

The choice of unit matters because methicillin-resistant \emph{S.
aureus} in this population is a clustered transmission process that a cross-sectional proportion cannot by itself characterize. For two decades, community MRSA in men who have
sex with men has been documented not as steady-state carriage but as
clustered, outbreak-pattern transmission --- the USA300 lineage moving
through sexual networks in San Francisco, Tokyo, and Chicago
\cite{diep2008, dejong2025}; outside those clusters colonization in MSM
is low, often near zero, and identifying as MSM is not itself a risk
factor. A prevalence proportion read at a single visit therefore swings
with whether a sample happens to catch a cluster (the trial carriage
trajectories, non-monotone and arm-crossing, show exactly this;
S1 Text), which is why the trial's individual-level line
matters. Carriage behaves like a clustered prevalence --- oscillating with sampling --- while the participant-level incident-resistance estimand accumulates first detections over follow-up, and a between-arms
contrast at one visit cannot see the second cleanly. What the trials
measured, and where, is a bounded phenomenon: MRSA clustering within the
sexual networks they enrolled to study.

But \emph{S. aureus} does not stay where MRSA clusters. The organism
colonizing these networks is the same one carried on skin throughout the
community, and resistance selected within the network could, if transmitted, extend beyond that boundary. The guidance that recommends doxy-PEP registers this
without instrumenting it. The CDC's 2024 recommendation names
antimicrobial resistance in ``other common bacterial pathogens (e.g.,
\emph{Staphylococcus aureus})'' as a concern, reproduces the trial
finding of an increase in tetracycline-resistant \emph{S. aureus} ``from
5\% \ldots{} to 13\%'' among colonized participants in the doxy-PEP arm
while the standard-of-care arm did not change, and concludes that
``potential risks related to the development of resistance and changes
in the microbiome will need to be monitored as these guidelines are
implemented'' \cite{cdc2024doxypep} --- while establishing no mechanism
by which such monitoring occurs. The signal is not concealed; it is
named, in the correct unit, by the trials and the federal guidance
alike, and made no one's measurement mandate. This is why we take
\emph{S. aureus}, not its methicillin-resistant fraction, as the unit of
analysis. Tetracycline susceptibility can matter clinically before
methicillin status is known: doxycycline is one of a small number of
oral options for outpatient skin and soft-tissue infection
\cite{stevens2014, liu2011}, and that choice is often made empirically,
before any methicillin result returns. Surveillance restricted to the
MRSA axis therefore answers along a dimension the treating decision does
not use, and the population that inherits the consequence is not the
trial cohort but everyone who later needs an oral agent against a
staphylococcal infection.

One scope condition follows immediately, and we state it at the outset
rather than let it surface as a late concession. Although \emph{S.
aureus} is the unit, much of the empirical material for how this
organism clusters and moves through these networks --- the
between-cohort dispersion, the outbreak jurisdictions that anchor the
guidance sample --- exists only for its methicillin-resistant subset,
because that subset is the only part any system was built to observe.
Where our own analysis is therefore forced onto MRSA we mark it as such;
the reliance is not an exception to the argument but an early instance
of it, and we return to what it costs in the Discussion.

The question this paper asks is therefore not whether doxy-PEP selects
for tetracycline resistance in \emph{S. aureus}, but whether the systems
assembled around doxy-PEP could detect it if it did. We examine the
three places an answer would have to come from. First, the trials:
whether \emph{S. aureus} was measured uniformly enough, at sufficient
power and duration, to resolve a selection signal, or whether it was
measured non-uniformly, sub-populated, and stopped early. Second, the
guidance: whether the documents that now govern doxy-PEP prescribing
require \emph{S. aureus} to be monitored, or, as the CDC's language
shows, invoke monitoring without instituting it. Third, surveillance:
whether any established system links doxycycline exposure to
staphylococcal phenotype at a population denominator. Each of these is a
place an answer would have to come from, and each is examined in turn
below.

Whether such unanswerability would matter depends on who would bear the
cost, and it is not the population being prescribed doxy-PEP. The
benefit is enjoyed by recipients; the resistance the intervention may
select, once loose in the community \emph{S. aureus} reservoir, is
inherited by whoever next needs an oral anti-staphylococcal agent ---
the outpatient with a purulent skin infection, and at the sharp end the
patient on dialysis, recovering from surgery, or receiving chemotherapy.
None is party to the prescribing decision, enrolled in the networks the
trials measured, or counted by any surveillance now in place. This is
the structure of a negative externality, and therefore a
question of distributive justice --- but the distinctive
feature, and the one this paper is about, is that the two sides are not
observed on comparable terms. The benefit has an identified exposed
population, an explicit denominator, prespecified endpoints, repeated
ascertainment, and randomised inference; the potential externality has
diffuse recipients, no exposure linkage, no corresponding phenotype at a
population denominator, and no assigned monitoring. The
measurement architecture makes one side of the benefit--externality
ledger estimable and leaves the other structurally under-observed. (Fig~\ref{fig1}) The
harm is not unmeasurable in principle --- the closing subsection of the
Discussion specifies the instrument that would measure it --- but
unpriced by the systems deployed, which is the stronger
governance claim, not the weaker. Establishing this requires more than
naming it; it requires showing, system by system, where the signal is
lost before it can reach anyone positioned to act. We therefore examine
each in turn --- the trials, the guidance, and the surveillance.

\section*{Results}

We coded the evidence base against a single yardstick --- what
would it take for this source to detect the bystander signal? ---
across three streams: the clinical trials (A), the guidelines built on
them (B), and the population surveillance meant to watch them (C).
Coding schemas, double-coding reliability, and every locator are in the
Methods and the repository; each source is SHA-256--pinned.

\subsection*{Stream A --- Can the clinical trials resolve the bystander-resistance signal?}


When the analysis was framed to match the question, the signal the
interim's cross-sectional presentation had not resolved appeared. In the
final DoxyPEP report the investigators ran the participant-level test
the interim had not --- a randomized comparison restricted to the
as-randomised follow-up (standard-of-care participants censored at
crossover): among those free of doxycycline-resistant \emph{S. aureus}
at baseline, time to first detection by arm, by Cox proportional
hazards. Doxy-PEP carried a 3.89-fold higher hazard of
acquiring doxycycline-resistant \emph{S. aureus} (95\% CI 1.42--10.68;
68 of 393 versus 5 of 163; p=0.0044), while the hazard of
clearing colonization did not differ between arms (HR 1.01,
0.69--1.46, p=0.98) \cite{luetkemeyer2025}. The effect was, in the
authors' own word, mixed: carriage unchanged, the hazard of incident resistant \emph{S. aureus} roughly quadrupled. By the falsification standard set out at the end of the
Discussion, the claim that this endpoint lay beyond the trial's reach is
refuted --- the trial reached it. What it did not, and by design could
not, reach is everything downstream of that individual-level fact, and
that is where the rest of this stream, and the paper, now turn. The trial's effect against the within-exposed reference band, and the downstream detection thresholds no deployed system reaches, are summarized in Fig~\ref{fig2}.

The sharper reading is historical, because the underlying resistance phenotype did not change
--- the question asked of it did. The 2023 interim reported a
cross-sectional resistance fraction and read it as reassurance (``modest
\ldots{} unlikely clinical significance''); the 2024 federal guideline
repeated a rise from 5\% to 13\% among the colonized and appended a call
for monitoring \cite{cdc2024doxypep}; the 2025 final analysis asked the
estimand the others had not --- incident resistance among those who
began without it, as a time-to-event contrast --- and returned HR 3.89
\cite{luetkemeyer2025}. Across three years the trial's own measurement
system evolved until it resolved its internal question --- an instance
not of measuring badly but of ordinary scientific self-correction, the
estimand catching up to the concern. The implementation system did not
evolve with it, and that gap is the subject of Streams B and C.

That evolution is legible in the interim record the final analysis
superseded, in features that recur --- generalized --- across the
stream. The first is a unit mismatch. The trial's data table is headed
``phenotypic resistance to the tetracycline antibiotic class,''
while its methods define the \emph{S. aureus} assay as
doxycycline (E-test, MIC ≥16 µg/mL): the assay measures one drug,
the label names a class, and the CDC guidance then inherits the wording,
describing this doxycycline result as ``tetracycline resistance in
\emph{S. aureus}'' \cite{cdc2024doxypep}. Because those are not
interchangeable phenotypes (below), an endpoint scored as ``the
tetracycline class'' does not answer the treatment-relevant question ---
a concrete instance of a downstream coding that does not match the
construct it is asked to carry.

The second feature is that the resistance signal was reported over
differing, incompletely documented denominators within the trial record,
and then propagated into secondary clinical reporting under additional
contrasts and conditioning sets. The sequence below follows that
transformation from the primary trial record into federal guidance and
clinical synthesis.

Within the trial's own venues, the resistant numerator changes from
5 to 16 to 28, the denominator basis shifts from colonised carriers to
all swabbed, and the two CROI presentations remain unreconciled in the
public record \cite{luetkemeyer2023, luetkemeyer2023croi,
cdc2024doxypep}. As the signal moved into secondary clinical synthesis,
its representation changed again: Mayer et al.\ reported a 5\% versus
4\% between-arm comparison without the underlying numerator,
denominator, timepoint, or conditioning set and described the finding
as ``infection'' \cite{mayer2023}, whereas Flores et al.\ reported a
5\% to 16\% within-arm increase conditioned on \emph{S. aureus}
isolation \cite{flores2025}. These expressions need not contradict one
another because they answer different questions. The metascientific
point is that the percentage travelled more reliably than the
denominator, contrast, conditioning set, and measurement terminology
needed to interpret it.

Part of the variation reflects different denominator bases and
contrasts; part reflects genuine differences in the reported counts that
cannot be reconciled from the public record. Resolving those differences
would require the individual-level data, and the third feature is that
individual-level data that could reconcile these reports are not publicly
available \cite{luetkemeyer2023}.

The reporting instability did not stop at the trial boundary.
Secondary clinical syntheses propagated the same safety finding in
additional, non-equivalent frames. Mayer et al.\ described participants
receiving doxy-PEP as more likely to become ``infected with
tetracycline-resistant \emph{S. aureus},'' 5\% versus 4\% in the control
group, without reporting the underlying numerator, denominator,
conditioning set, or timepoint \cite{mayer2023}. Flores et al.\ instead
reported, among participants with nasopharyngeal \emph{S. aureus}
isolated, an increase within the doxy-PEP arm from 5\% at baseline to
16\% after 12 months \cite{flores2025}. These statements need not be
numerically contradictory: they condition on and contrast different
populations. That is precisely the reporting problem. As the result
moved from trial report to clinical synthesis, the percentage travelled
more reliably than the denominator, contrast, and measurement unit
needed to interpret it.

Nor is the bystander the only under-measured organism here: even the
intensively surveilled gonococcus was phenotyped in fewer than one
diagnosis in five --- phenotypic resistance testing was unavailable for
83\% (212/256) of gonorrhea diagnoses --- and, having reported this, the
investigators called for longer-term monitoring
\cite{luetkemeyer2023croi}. The rest of the stream asks whether that
monitoring is possible with the evidence base as it stands.

The trials usually said to have ``assayed \emph{S. aureus}'' did so on
incompatible terms: DoxyPEP measured doxycycline resistance within \emph{S. aureus} by E-test over
two disagreeing denominators; DuDHS measured the same phenotype by disc
diffusion in a single-digit number of carriers; DOXYVAC measured only
MRSA carriage prevalence --- a methicillin, not a tetracycline,
quantity (and one that itself rose within the doxy-PEP arm, 1.8\% to
9.9\%, while being reported as a between-arms null
\cite{vanbaelen2024c}). Of three trials, two measured the phenotype
this question turns on, by two methods over three denominators, and none
resolves mechanism; a pooled estimate is not defensible, and the
heterogeneity is itself the finding.

The trials' cross-sectional carriage endpoints also cannot resolve the
transmission structure the concern actually concerns. \emph{S. aureus}
carriage in these networks is clustered, and a cross-sectional
prevalence read at these small counts cannot separate clustered
transmission from sampling noise: in both pivotal trials the carriage
trajectories move non-monotonically and in step across arms; a naive
between-arm trend test, simulated against the documented between-cohort
dispersion of MRSA colonization, is badly oversized (scoring a flat
process as a significant trend most of the time); and the outbreak
curvature that would mark clustering is statistically unidentifiable
from a plain trend. The significant signal the final analysis returned
is an individual-level incidence hazard, and says nothing either
way about whether resistant strains cluster or propagate. The full
carriage-trajectory, dispersion, trend-test-degradation, and
outbreak-shape analyses are in the S1 Text and regenerate
from the repository (\texttt{outputs/}); they establish that the cross-sectional endpoint could not resolve the downstream population question under the observed designs and sampling structure, and are not load-bearing for the individual-level
result now in hand. Where interim summaries emphasized the
methicillin-resistant subset --- in which resistance stayed low (MRSA
about 6\% of sampled participants, doxy-R MRSA unchanged) --- over the
broader \emph{S. aureus} measure, in which it did not, the two reporting
frames foregrounded different units of analysis and therefore supported
different apparent interpretations; the final incidence analysis has
since resolved which one the exposure moved.

That the interim read as null was not a matter of sample size alone:
its cross-sectional contrasts were underpowered for any externally
specified effect (0 of 4 powered; minimum detectable RR 3.8--5.1 against
Soge's RR ≈ 1.42--2.25 \cite{soge2025}; S1 Text), but
the final analysis did not simply add data --- it changed what was
measured, from a cross-sectional resistance proportion to
participant-level incident resistance. Its hazard ratio is
therefore not comparable to that cross-sectional minimum-detectable
effect; the measurement design, not the sample, became adequate
to the question. What no change of estimand can do is reach past the
trial's roughly one-year horizon.

A last feature is mechanistic, and it is the measurement point at its
finest grain. Tetracycline resistance in \emph{S. aureus} is not one
phenotype: efflux (tet(K)) and ribosomal-protection (tet(M)) mechanisms
differ in whether doxycycline still works, and MRSA status measures
methicillin resistance, not tetracycline resistance at all
\cite{liu2011, grossman2016}. The trial resolved the doxycycline
phenotype (HR 3.89) but not its molecular basis, and a standard
breakpoint cannot separate the two --- so the choice of surveillance
variable determines which downstream question remains answerable. (A
cross-organism instance of the same blindness --- a military
antimalarial-doxycycline cohort in which overall resistance did not
differ by exposure yet tet(M) genes were significantly more common in
the exposed --- is in the S1 Text.)

What the trial resolved is therefore bounded, and naming the boundary is
what sets up the rest of the paper. It established that, over about a
year of randomised exposure, participants who began free of
doxycycline-resistant \emph{S. aureus} were roughly four times as likely
to have it detected. It did not establish whether that resistance
persists under longer exposure; whether it is tet(K), tet(M), tet(L), or
tet(38), or how that molecular architecture shifts over time; whether
the risk scales with doxycycline dose; whether resistant lineages spread
from doxy-PEP recipients to others or propagate through sexual and
community networks; whether population-level \emph{S. aureus} resistance
moves as a result; whether any of it yields more resistant clinical
infections; or what happens past the roughly twelve-month,
three-timepoint horizon --- nares and oropharynx sampled at baseline,
six, and twelve months, which is a trial procedure, not population
surveillance. Each of these is a population-level or durational question
on which the clinical stake actually rests, and the trial, by design,
reaches none of them. The randomised instrument generated the signal;
whether the deployed architecture inherited the capacity to follow it is
a separate question --- the one Streams B and C answer.

\subsection*{Stream B ---Do clinical guidelines convert the AMR concern into a measurement mandate?}

The governmental guidance we coded shows the same within-document
asymmetry throughout: the efficacy question receives a graded,
systematic evidence review; the antimicrobial-resistance harm ---
explicitly including resistant-pathogen development --- receives an
ungraded literature review in the same document. Providers are
required to counsel patients about resistance in commensal
organisms, yet no document requires any \emph{S. aureus}
measurement, follow-up culture, or bystander panel; where a monitoring
recommendation appears at all, it concerns a different organism.

To make the denominator principled rather than a convenience sample, we
anchor it to the same evidence that establishes the clustering in Stream
A: the US jurisdictions with documented CA-MRSA/USA300 transmission in
MSM (de Jong 2025 and the outbreak literature it cites
\cite{dejong2025, diep2008}). Of the six documented-outbreak
jurisdictions with governmental doxy-PEP guidance --- San Francisco,
Chicago, New York City, Boston (Massachusetts), Los Angeles County, and
San Diego --- 0 require or suggest \emph{S. aureus} monitoring.
The finding survives an independence check applied to the six from
document content: four are independently authored (San Francisco,
Chicago, NYC, San Diego), one (LA County) is adapted from San Francisco
and one (Massachusetts) from an external template, so the declines are
not one posture copied across departments. Nor is it for want of naming
the organism --- three of the six name \emph{S. aureus} specifically,
two invoke only a generic ``microbiome'' risk, and one is silent; even
where it is named, none measure it. A further jurisdiction whose
guidance simply defers to CDC inherits CDC's non-monitoring and is
counted separately, not as an independent decline. Across the full coded
corpus (10 documents; CDC, WHO, and eight US jurisdictions) the result
is identical: 0 instrument the bystander. Inter-coder agreement was
92\%; the one field with meaningful disagreement (κ = 0.58) is reported
as such and not rounded up.


\subsection*{Stream C --- Can deployed surveillance link doxy-PEP exposure to the relevant S. aureus phenotype?}

The monitoring the trial's authors call for would have to link two
things at a common population denominator: who is exposed to doxy-PEP,
and what tetracycline phenotype \emph{S. aureus} carries. Across a
closed universe of twelve established US surveillance systems
(governmental only; commercial and research datasets and provider
directories excluded with reasons), none does --- and the decomposition
says why. Two systems capture exposure at a population denominator ---
AIDSVu and CDC AtlasPlus, both as a PrEP proxy, not doxy-PEP
itself \cite{aidsvu} --- and zero capture \emph{S. aureus}
tetracycline resistance at a population denominator. The join is
vacuous: there is nothing on the phenotype side to join to. It is not
that two halves exist and no one connects them; one half does not exist.

The reason it does not exist is a codified seam. Wherever \emph{S.
aureus} is surveilled at a population denominator, the reported axis is
methicillin, not tetracycline: the one population-based platform,
ABCs/EIP, reports invasive MRSA; NHSN reports MRSA events; and
the only routine source of \emph{S. aureus} tetracycline susceptibility
--- the facility cumulative antibiogram --- is non-standardized and
carries no population denominator, because CLSI M39, the national
standard, mandates the MRSA/MSSA split and makes tetracycline a
selectively suppressible supplemental agent. NARMS, the closest-named
federal AR system, excludes \emph{S. aureus} altogether;
tetracycline-resistant \emph{S. aureus} is nationally notifiable
nowhere. The surveillance architecture is organized around the
methicillin axis at every tier, and the tetracycline unit is orthogonal
to it --- the Stream C twin of the endpoint substitution that runs
through Stream A. The two sides also exist at incompatible resolutions:
exposure at county/metro, phenotype only at denominator-free facility
level, so no geography carries both. None of this says the linkage is
impossible in principle --- a system could be built, most directly by
adding tetracycline susceptibility for \emph{S. aureus} to the
population-based ABCs/EIP platform that already holds the denominator
--- only that none is deployed. (Fig~\ref{fig3}.)

Nor could an ordinary population system recover the missing side simply
by collecting more observations. Under realistic exposure density and
isolate volumes, the within-exposed effect required to move a population
rate runs to roughly RR 14--64 --- far above any plausible
effect (Soge's optimistic 1.42--2.25) --- and the only configuration
that restores sensitivity is to sample \emph{S. aureus} directly from
doxy-PEP-exposed persons, which converts the system into a sentinel
cohort rather than surveillance (detailed sensitivity analysis,
including the state and metro grids and the proxy-free break-even, in
Figures A--C in S1 Text). The failure is
therefore not merely that a variable is missing: the two sides of the
intervention ledger occupy different evidentiary systems. Doxy-PEP
benefit is measured in an identified exposed population; a
population-level resistance consequence would be borne by a broader
population whose \emph{S. aureus} tetracycline phenotype is linked to
exposure at no common denominator --- and because the mismatch is one of
unit, not sample size, scaling the inherited instrument does not
repair it. (Fig~\ref{fig3}.)

\subsection*{The literature's attention structure mirrors the instrumentation}

A defined PubMed search (2015--2026; queries and a frozen result
snapshot in the repository) identified 361 doxy-PEP papers, of which 13
(3.6\%) mention a bystander staphylococcal organism and only 2 measure
tetracycline-resistant \emph{S. aureus} against a doxy-PEP exposure
contrast. The bystander is therefore rarely mentioned and, when
mentioned, rarely measured
against exposure. This is not evidence of neglect by any author; it is
consistent with a research system whose attention remains concentrated
where its existing instruments already point. The literature's attention
structure mirrors---and is consistent with inheritance of---the
intervention's target-organism frame, just as the surveillance
architecture does. The visibility asymmetry is thus present across four
layers: trial measurement and reporting, guidance, deployed surveillance,
and the research attention that might reveal gaps among them.

\subsection*{Across streams, observability is asymmetric between intervention and externality}

The three streams share a structure, and that structure is itself a
result. The intervention's benefit and the trial's resistance signal are
identifiable within an exposed population --- exposure ascertained,
denominator defined, phenotype measured, a longitudinal causal contrast
available. The population that could inherit a downstream resistance
consequence has none of these jointly: no assigned measurement mandate
(Stream B) and no deployed system in which exposure and the \emph{S.
aureus} tetracycline phenotype share a denominator (Stream C), with the
same asymmetry extending into what the literature makes observable at all (Table~\ref{table1}).

signal are scientifically legible within the exposed population, while a

visibility distributive observability --- who remains
represented by the evidence system, and with what inferential
resolution, once the scientific question moves downstream. It does not
establish that a population harm has occurred. It establishes that, were
such a harm occurring, the deployed evidence architecture could estimate
the intervention side of the benefit--externality ledger far more
completely than the population consequence side.

\section*{Discussion}

\subsection*{Measurement inheritance}

We asked not whether doxy-PEP drives population-level resistant \emph{S.
aureus}, but whether the systems assembled around doxy-PEP could follow
such a signal if the trial found one. The trial has now found one --- a
significant increase in incident doxycycline-resistant \emph{S. aureus}
under exposure (HR 3.89), with no matching effect on carriage --- and
its authors close by calling for public-health surveillance of AMR in
\emph{S. aureus} and other common colonisers \cite{luetkemeyer2025}.
The upstream instrument has generated the information; the question is
whether the downstream architecture can receive it, and across the two
places that would have to --- guidance and surveillance --- the answer
is no.

The sharper reading is that the trial's own measurement system evolved
to meet its internal question while the implementation system did not.
The 2023 interim read a cross-sectional fraction as reassurance; the
2024 federal guideline appended a call for monitoring; the 2025 final
analysis asked the estimand the others had not --- incident resistance
among those who began without it --- and returned HR 3.89. Three years,
one underlying phenotype, an evolving estimand --- an instance of ordinary
scientific self-correction --- and at the end of it a demonstrated
signal handed to systems built to measure something else. The
load-bearing observation still belongs to the trial team, not to us: the
surveillance they call for is surveillance the evidence base they helped
launch is not built to provide.

This is measurement inheritance. An analysis --- or a guideline,
or a surveillance system --- carries in its sampling frame, cadence,
coding, denominator, and unit the organism and question it was built
for. The doxy-PEP apparatus was built to measure gonococcal efficacy;
applied to a bystander commensal it inherits a denominator the
intervention itself moves, a phenotype label that spans two mechanisms,
and a surveillance architecture standardized around the methicillin axis
while the tetracycline unit falls orthogonally through it. The
downstream gap is not an oversight closable by a better analysis of the
trial's data --- that analysis has now been done, and it is significant;
it is a property of the guidance and surveillance instruments, built
around a different organism and a different question.

Measurement inheritance is built from familiar parts but reducible to
none of them. Construct validity asks whether a measure
represents its intended construct; estimand mismatch, whether the
quantity estimated matches the question posed \cite{montoya2025estimand}; ascertainment and
surveillance bias, who or what is systematically observed \cite{tancredi2024surveillance};
metric-substitution problems of the Goodhart and McNamara type,
what happens when an available metric stands in for the thing of interest \cite{treem2023visibility}. These concepts generally diagnose mismatch within a measurement, analysis, or evaluation stage. Measurement inheritance instead concerns how such mismatches propagate across stages of an evidence-production chain: the question changes as responsibility passes
downstream, but the downstream system retains the unit, coding,
denominator, cadence, or linkage inherited from the upstream question
--- so the very features that made the measure valid upstream render it
blind downstream. The contribution is not to name any constituent bias
but to show that they can be coupled across a chain, and to
convert that observation into a per-source, falsifiable detectability
standard another meta-researcher could apply to a different intervention
and its bystander.

\subsection*{Distributive observability}

That coupling has a distributive consequence, and it is a
measurement asymmetry before it is a moral one. The
intervention's benefit and its potential downstream cost accrue to different populations, and only one is observed. The benefit is measured
in an identified exposed population --- exposure ascertained,
denominator defined, phenotype measured, a longitudinal causal contrast
available; the potential resistance externality would be borne by the
outpatient with a skin infection and, at the sharp end, the dialysis,
surgical, and oncology patients for whom an oral anti-staphylococcal
agent is no convenience --- none party to the prescribing decision,
enrolled in the networks the trials measured, or counted by any deployed
system. The distributive core of the finding is therefore not that one
population benefits at another's expense --- no such population-level
harm has been established --- but that the two sides are rendered
epistemically unequal: the evidence architecture permits one side of the
ledger to be estimated far more completely than the other. A negative
externality that no instrument can price is not thereby small; it is
simply unweighable, and an evidence architecture that estimates the intervention side of the ledger far more completely than the externality side cannot establish that benefit outweighs external cost. That asymmetry in
what can be known is itself a finding about how research systems
allocate visibility.

The structure is not peculiar to doxy-PEP. It can arise wherever an
intervention's benefits are concentrated in an observed recipient
population while its consequences diffuse beyond it --- antimicrobial
ecological effects, delayed treatment toxicities, microbiome
perturbation, pathogen evolution, environmental pharmaceutical exposure,
community transmission, and deployed algorithmic harms all share the
shape. Measurement inheritance predicts a recurrent failure mode in such
settings: downstream consequences become hardest to evaluate precisely
when their unit of harm diverges from the unit around which the original
evidence system was built. Doxy-PEP is a worked instance in which the
data license every transition of that logic; it is not the boundary of the concept. And because guidance and surveillance are the systems that shape clinical practice and health policy, a measurement failure that survives these transitions is not a documentation problem but a propagating one --- the domino effect by which weaknesses in the evidence base reach patient care and policy \cite{armond2024integrity}.

\subsection*{What the trial leaves biologically unresolved}

Two of the missing coordinates are worth naming, because they show how
fine the grain of the problem is. The first is mechanism: a single
categorical ``tetracycline resistance'' measure can collapse phenotypes
with different clinical meanings --- efflux (tet(K)) leaves doxycycline
largely usable, ribosomal protection (tet(M)) can compromise doxycycline and minocycline activity \cite{liu2011, grossman2016} --- and the trial
resolved incident doxycycline resistance without resolving which;
whether the resistant fraction is enriching for the class-spanning
determinant cannot be read off a single breakpoint. We do not forecast
which mechanism is spreading; the point is that an inherited
surveillance variable can remain valid for the question it was designed
to answer while being insufficient for the new one imposed on it. The
second is our own reliance on MRSA: we take \emph{S. aureus}, not its
methicillin-resistant fraction, as the unit, yet much of the evidence
for how this organism moves through these networks is MRSA --- because
no \emph{S. aureus}-tetracycline dataset of clustering, transmission, or
population phenotype exists to reason from. That reliance is an instance
of measurement inheritance, not an exception to it: we are forced onto
the only part of the organism any system was built to see, which is the
lower bound of what can presently be known. Colonization is also not
infection, and the colonization--infection link is itself unmeasured
here --- one more thing the apparatus does not see.

\subsection*{What instrument would answer the question}

Naming the missing coordinates specifies the instrument. Sentinel
sampling of \emph{S. aureus} from doxy-PEP-exposed individuals raises
the exposed fraction by construction and fixes the denominator;
resistance reported per carrier and by mechanism (tet(M) versus tet(K))
rather than as a pooled ``tetracycline-class'' rate; dosing frequency
rather than prescription status; a surveillance feed carrying \emph{S.
aureus} tetracycline susceptibility at a population denominator, most
directly by adding it to the population-based platform that already
tracks invasive MRSA and joining it to exposure on geography; and, at a
minimum, release of the individual-level data the trials already hold.
None is exotic; none is yet in place at scale. That absence, rather than
any single estimate, is the finding.

\subsection*{Falsification, limits, and what we do not claim}

We do not claim that doxy-PEP drives population-level resistant \emph{S.
aureus}, or that the individual-level signal the trial found propagates
into a population externality --- that is precisely what no deployed
system can now determine. The trial's own randomised analysis of
incident doxycycline-resistant \emph{S. aureus} is statistically
significant (HR 3.89), and we accept it rather than dispute it; the
earlier per-carrier reconstruction we used to argue the interim
understated the change is retired in its favor. We claim that the
guidance and surveillance built around doxy-PEP are not instrumented to
follow that signal to the population level where the clinical stake
lies, and that the interim's reassuring framing was an artifact of
estimand and instrumentation the final analysis has since corrected.

Consistent with a falsification standard, we set out in advance what
would defeat each leg. One leg has, in part, already been decided ---
and against our earlier framing, which we report plainly. We had
proposed that the argument would weaken if any primary trial proved
adequately powered for the bystander endpoint; the final DoxyPEP
analysis is, and its incident-resistance signal is significant (HR
3.89). We do not treat that as a weakening but as the paper's premise:
the upstream instrument worked. What it did not do --- resolve the
mechanism (tet(K) versus tet(M)), or follow the signal past twelve
months, or to the population --- is what the remaining legs concern. The
inheritance claim fails if a doxy-PEP guideline comes to require
\emph{S. aureus} monitoring (Stream B falsified), or if a deployed
surveillance system links doxy-PEP exposure to an \emph{S. aureus}
tetracycline phenotype at a common population denominator (Stream C
falsified). The underlying biological hypothesis remains separately
falsifiable: were tetracycline resistance in \emph{S. aureus} shown flat
or declining in high-exposure populations while negative controls
behaved, the concern would be wrong, and that too would be a publishable
result. The present claim is prior to and independent of that population
test: the trial has resolved the individual-level question, and with the
surveillance that exists the population question cannot be run at all.

\section*{Materials and methods}

\subsection*{The measurement-inheritance schema}

We make the construct operational so that it can be applied beyond this
case. Treat the answerability of a scientific question as requiring a
measurement vector with dimensions

\[M = \{\,U,\ E,\ P,\ D,\ T,\ L,\ R\,\}\]

where \(U\) is the unit or population, \(E\) the exposure, \(P\) the
phenotype or outcome, \(D\) the denominator, \(T\) the temporal
structure or cadence, \(L\) the linkage or granularity at which \(E\)
and \(P\) are joined, and \(R\) the locus of measurement responsibility
--- who is charged with observing \(P\). Measurement
inheritance occurs when a downstream evidence system retains dimensions
appropriate to the upstream question but not those required by
the downstream estimand --- so the question is unanswerable by
that system not for want of data but for want of an inherited dimension.
A distributive corollary follows when the intervention's benefit and its
potential downstream harm accrue to different populations, \(B \neq H\):
the beneficiary population \(B\) remains represented by the inherited
\(\{U,E,P,D,L\}\), while the population \(H\) that would bear an
externalised consequence is represented by none of them jointly; estimating an effect on $H$ would require an estimand defined on the affected rather than the treated population \cite{montoya2025affected}. We call that condition distributive observability --- a property of the
measurement system, not an ethical premise added to it. The three streams instantiate the schema directly, as Fig~\ref{fig1} renders stage by stage: the trial and its reporting inherited a cross-sectional carriage framing ($P$, $T$) where incident resistance (time-to-event $T$; participant $U$) was the quantity that mattered; guidance inherited efficacy evidence and counselling where an assigned measurement obligation for $P$ was needed; and surveillance inherited the methicillin axis ($P$) and population denominators ($D$) that never meet the tetracycline phenotype at a common linkage ($L$).



Read this way, each stream's failure is a specific missing coordinate of
\(M\), and the audit below is a check, source by source, of which
coordinates were inherited and which were not.

\subsection*{Design and sequential pipeline}

The study is a documentary and quasi-computational audit of whether the
doxy-PEP evidence base can detect the bystander signal, assembled in
three streams collected and analyzed in sequence: the clinical trials
(Stream A), the governmental guidance built on them (Stream B), and the
population surveillance meant to monitor them (Stream C). Each stream is
scored against one yardstick --- the within-exposed effect it could
detect --- so the three are commensurable. Collection was document-led
and iterative: sources were located, read, coded with locators, and
SHA-256--hashed before analysis, and decisions that could go either way
were logged with dates in \texttt{DECISIONS.md} before results were
known. No individual-level or restricted data were used; every input is
public, and every figure and table regenerates from a single command.

\subsection*{Sources and coding}

Primary trials, guidelines, and comparator papers were coded one
document per YAML record, every value carrying a page/section/table
locator; the builder fails on a missing locator rather than emitting a
null. The unit of analysis is \emph{S. aureus}; MSSA and MRSA are coded
as subsets, not as the endpoint (methicillin status is downstream of the
empirical prescribing decision, the Introduction). Resistance
observations are coded one per reported instance (not per
underlying result), recording the phenotype measured versus the
phenotype as labeled, the mechanism-discrimination method, the
numerator/denominator, the denominator basis (all-swabbed, carriers,
isolates), and the source's evidentiary status ---
\texttt{primary\_trial}, \texttt{conference\_abstract} (the trial's own
pre-publication data, e.g.~a CROI table, kept distinct so its figures
can be compared against the later publication rather than merged with
it), \texttt{guideline}, or \texttt{secondary\_synthesis}. Where a
trial's per-timepoint denominators are absent from the public record
(DOXYVAC's MRSA carriage; the main-trial paper is not in the corpus), no
observation is fabricated --- the gap is recorded and the source flagged
for acquisition. A cross-trial heterogeneity summary tabulates the axis,
assay, and denominator each trial used. Guidelines were double-coded (blind
second coder; 92\% agreement) with Cohen's κ per field. All 40+ source
files are SHA-256--pinned (\texttt{data/raw/**/CHECKSUMS.md}); the CROI
abstract's source and hash are recorded in
\texttt{data/raw/papers/SOURCES.md} and \texttt{CHECKSUMS.md} (the
transcription itself is a third-party work and is not redistributed).

\subsection*{Stream A --- the trials}

Detectability. For each primary-trial \emph{S. aureus} arm
comparison --- first-party published data only;
\texttt{conference\_abstract}, guideline, and secondary sources are
excluded so the power analysis rests on peer-reviewed denominators ---
we compute, from the arm sizes and observed control rate alone, the
exact-Fisher power to detect an effect fixed a priori from a
source external to the trials, and the minimum detectable relative risk
at 80\% power. The benchmark is the \emph{S. aureus}-matched
\(RR = 2.25\) (Soge et al.: tetracycline-resistant \emph{S. aureus}
colonization, 18\% versus 8\% in doxy-PEP users versus non-users); a
cross-organism gonococcal \(RR = 1.42\) is carried as a flagged
secondary. Fixing the effect externally is what makes this a
design-based sensitivity analysis, not observed power; per-arm
counts are single- to low-double-digit, so the exact test is used
throughout, the Fisher rejection region enumerated once per pair of arm
sizes.

Duration and sample accrual. To quantify what the trials' early
termination cost the bystander endpoint we extend the same frame:
preserving each comparison's arm ratio and control rate, we solve for
the sample that would reach 80\% power against the matched benchmark and
report the multiple over the sample realised. Because that required
sample lands in the hundreds to thousands per arm --- the regime where
the large-sample normal approximation is accurate and exact-region
enumeration is impractical --- the required \(N\) is computed by the
normal approximation and spot-checked against exact Fisher at the solved
denominators. The trials' \emph{S. aureus} series carries only two
arm-split follow-up visits with a declining per-visit denominator, which
cannot support a defensible extrapolation from sample to follow-up
months; we therefore report the sample multiple and do not infer a
duration, recording the early efficacy stop as the reason the realised
denominator is what it is rather than as a counterfactual about a longer
trial.

Selection. The interim publications reported no
participant-level test of selection, so an earlier version of this
analysis reconstructed one from aggregate counts --- recovering
susceptible carriage as colonization prevalence minus the all-swabbed
resistance rate, and testing the predicted depletion and per-carrier
increase directionally. The final DoxyPEP analysis has since performed
the appropriate participant-level test itself: among participants free
of doxycycline-resistant \emph{S. aureus} at baseline, time to first
detection by arm, standard-of-care censored at crossover, by Cox
proportional hazards \cite{luetkemeyer2025}. We therefore report that
randomised result directly (the Stream A results) and retire the
aggregate reconstruction, retaining the interim's two denominators only
as an exhibit of how the same endpoint was conveyed across venues before
the incidence estimand resolved it. We fit no shape to the short
arm-split series.

Clustering. That MRSA behaves as a clustered, network
phenomenon rather than a quantity a cross-sectional proportion can
settle is instrumented separately and kept MRSA-scoped, since MRSA is a
subset and not the analytic unit. We first estimate the empirical
overdispersion of MRSA colonization prevalence across the independent
MSM cohorts assembled by a systematic review \cite{dejong2025} --- the
same review whose outbreak sources define the Stream B denominator
(Materials and methods) --- as a logit-normal binomial random-effects
model fitted by marginal maximum likelihood (Gauss--Hermite quadrature),
reporting the between-cohort dispersion on the logit scale with a
profile-likelihood interval and treating it as a deliberately
conservative external sensitivity anchor for the scale of
heterogeneity (not an estimate of the trial's between-visit variance),
since the ten colonization cohorts (of eighteen studies screened, the
ten reporting usable prevalence) cannot partition genuine clustering
from the methodological heterogeneity it also absorbs. Against that
dispersion we ask, by simulation at a trial's actual per-visit
denominators, what the naive cross-sectional trend test a between-arm
reading relies on (Cochran--Armitage, binomial variance) does as
clustering grows --- its power to detect a real trend, and its
false-positive rate under a flat process --- anchoring the reported
figures at the estimated dispersion and its lower confidence bound.
Separately, we fit flat, monotone-trend, and outbreak-wave models to the
observed MRSA carriage series by maximum likelihood and compare them by
AIC and by parametric-bootstrap likelihood-ratio tests calibrated under
the reduced model, asking whether any rise is present and whether the
outbreak curvature is distinguishable from a plain trend. Both analyses
report null and calibration quantities --- an inability to distinguish,
a false-positive rate --- which a slide-read denominator cannot
manufacture; the one positive within-arm trend statistic in this
material is a published-figure reanalysis \cite{vanbaelen2024c}, cited
as such, not ours.

\subsection*{Stream B --- the guidance}

Denominator. The Stream B denominator is purposive rather than
a convenience set of whatever guidance was on hand, and its selection
rule is inherited from the paper's own clustering evidence: the US
jurisdictions with documented CA-MRSA/USA300 transmission or elevated
clustered colonization in MSM, as defined by de Jong's systematic review
and the primary outbreak literature it cites
\cite{dejong2025, diep2008} --- the same source that supplies the
clustering dispersion of Materials and methods. The jurisdictions where
the bystander concern is epidemiologically grounded are thus exactly the
jurisdictions whose guidance is assessed. We take governmental
public-health guidance only (state, county, municipal); clinic and
provider-organisation documents are excluded on the stated ground that
they carry neither surveillance authority nor monitoring policy,
retained with that reason rather than dropped silently. Each outbreak
jurisdiction is classed as matched (guidance located and coded),
a gap (no governmental guidance locatable --- itself a finding,
resolved to located-and-coded or confirmed-none before the sample
closes), or, for on-disk jurisdictions outside the outbreak set,
retained separately under a high-exposure rather than
documented-outbreak rationale.

Coding. Each document is coded from full text, not search
snippets --- establishing that a document does not monitor the organism
requires reading it --- one document per record, every finding carrying
a locator. The schema captures a within-artifact asymmetry: whether the
document mandates in-category monitoring (gonorrhoea, chlamydia,
syphilis) and orders host-toxicity labs, set against whether it names,
counsels, or measures \emph{S. aureus}. Bystander treatment is graded
silent, generic microbiome/resistance, or organism-named; \emph{S.
aureus} monitoring as required, suggested, silent, or explicitly none,
the last requiring a verbatim quote. Coding is done blind to the
expected outcome --- the expectation that no document exceeds patient
counselling is exactly where confirmation would let an edge case slip
--- with any departure flagged rather than normalized. A blinded second
coder double-codes with per-field Cohen's κ.

Independence. Because the headline counts jurisdictions that
decline to monitor, we separate independent assessments from echoes. A
document that defers to CDC --- reproducing its guidance without local
policy --- inherits CDC's non-monitoring and is a dependent observation,
coded and reported apart from independent declines. The same test is
then applied within the outbreak-matched set, from document
content --- structure, section order, verbatim or near-verbatim shared
passages, and stated derivation (``adapted from''): documents adapted
from a shared template or from one another are grouped as one lineage,
so the count of genuinely independent declines is reported as such and
not inflated by replication.

\subsection*{Stream C --- the surveillance}

Stream C asks the linkage question directly: does any deployed US
public-health surveillance system link doxy-PEP exposure to \emph{S.
aureus} tetracycline/doxycycline resistance at a common population
denominator? We enumerate a closed universe of established systems
(federal, state, or metro) that plausibly capture, at a population
denominator, either doxy-PEP/doxycycline exposure or \emph{S. aureus}
antimicrobial-resistance phenotype --- the same governmental-only
discipline as Stream B, excluding commercial or research datasets and
provider directories with a stated reason --- and code each on three
capabilities: exposure-side capture, phenotype-side capture (of the
\emph{S. aureus} tetracycline unit, not the MRSA subset), and the join.
The result is reported decomposed --- how many systems capture
exposure only, phenotype only, both-but-unlinked, or link the two ---
and the geographic resolution at which each side exists is recorded,
since a resolution mismatch can foreclose the join even in principle. As
in Streams A and B, MRSA/methicillin systems are coded as a flagged
wrong-unit near-miss, not the unit, and every claim is bounded to
deployed systems: a system that could detect the signal is
specified affirmatively, so the gap reads as an un-built instrument
rather than an unmeasurable phenomenon.

A supporting analysis asks whether, even at a common population
denominator, the exposed subgroup is dense enough to move a population
rate. The dilution fraction is \(f =
\phi\,(\text{male-PrEP rate}/10^5)\,\text{uptake}\,\kappa\),
population-independent because it tracks exposure density, not
headcount; the minimum detectable change in a proportion near \(R_0\)
(two-sided α = 0.05, 80\% power) inverts to \(RR_\text{needed} = 1 +
\text{MDE}/(f R_0)\). Parameters are bounded to favor detection
so a failed gate is robust: uptake ∈ \{20, 35, 55\}\%, κ ∈ \{0.2, 0.5,
1, 3, 5\} (κ \textless{} 1 is the realistic region --- the exposed are
under-represented in a population isolate stream), \(R_0\) ∈ \{5, 10,
13\}\%, isolate volume \(N\) ∈ \{1 000, 10 000, 100 000\} per
geography-year (100 000 exceeds any real US stream). A panel-power bound
and a break-even frontier in proxy-free units (share of all adult males
on PrEP) are computed identically; the metro case uses Washington, D.C.
(2 694 per 100 000 adult males) as the observed density ceiling because
no metro/county PrEP density exists as a public isolate-linked resource.

\subsection*{Reproducibility}

All analysis is Python 3.11 (pandas, statsmodels, scipy, matplotlib); no
proprietary dependencies. Raw data are immutable; all cleaning is in
code; every figure and table regenerates from \texttt{make\ all}.
Analysis decisions that could go either way are logged with dates in
\texttt{DECISIONS.md} before results are known. The repository is public with code and data. These practices --- immutable sources, code-based cleaning, dated pre-result decision logging, and one-command regeneration --- are among the transparency and reproducibility measures identified as supporting research reliability and integrity \cite{armond2024integrity}.
