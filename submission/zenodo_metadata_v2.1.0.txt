DESCRIPTION / ABSTRACT
Reproducible research compendium (code, coded source data, manuscript, and validated outputs) for a three-stream evidence audit of doxycycline post-exposure prophylaxis (doxy-PEP) and bystander tetracycline-resistant Staphylococcus aureus. The pivotal DoxyPEP trial's final analysis reported a significant increase in incident doxycycline-resistant S. aureus under exposure (HR 3.89, 95% CI 1.42-10.68), and its authors called for surveillance of it. The compendium asks whether the guidance and surveillance deployed around doxy-PEP could follow that signal. The audit is explicitly scoped to the United States: no governmental guideline requires S. aureus monitoring (0 of 6 outbreak-matched jurisdictions; 0 of 10 coded, the World Health Organization included only as the international apex reference), and none of 12 established US surveillance and data systems links doxy-PEP exposure to an S. aureus tetracycline phenotype at a common population denominator (0 of 12). Under the primary combined PrEP+PLWH exposed denominator (AIDSVu 2024), an ordinary population system would need an implausible within-exposed effect to move a population rate (RR approximately 6-28; approximately 14-64 under the more restrictive PrEP-only indexing). England's 2026 UK Health Security Agency doxy-PEP monitoring-and-evaluation plan is discussed as an external comparator that names the same bystander-resistance objective, illustrating the additional surveillance structure the US lacks. The upstream instrument generated information the downstream systems are not built to receive - a gap we term measurement inheritance. No population-level causal claim is made; contains no participant-level trial data. Every figure and table regenerates by a single command and all coded sources are SHA-256-pinned.

This version (v2.1.0) refocuses the manuscript as an explicitly US surveillance audit and adds England's 2026 UKHSA doxy-PEP monitoring-and-evaluation plan as external supporting evidence (prose only; not added to the coded corpus, which retains the World Health Organization as the international apex reference). It corrects a benchmark-labelling error in the superseded feasibility code (Soge's S. aureus-matched relative risk is 2.25 from 18% vs 8%; the value 1.42 is the cross-organism gonococcal figure), consolidates figure generation to a single authoritative writer per shipped figure, adds a dose-threshold sensitivity analysis and combined-model tests, and hardens `make all` reproducibility. No frozen scientific result changed. It supersedes v2.0.0.
